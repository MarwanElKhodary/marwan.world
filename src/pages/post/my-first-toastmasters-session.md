## Header

Last week, I went to my first Toastmasters session at First Oakville Toastmasters.

## Why Toastmasters?

Why am I interested in Toastmasters in the first place? I consider myself a decently sociable person, lucky enough to have many friends and social activites every week. But as a remote worker, I have never had to stress about public speaking as a skill. I have presented, numerous times to Teams meetings with +30 participants, but it's not the same. Sitting at my desk talking about Azure architecture or microservices to one person who's on mute and no camera on is the same as talking to 30 people at once.

Now with the rise of AI, I've been thinking about my career outlook as a self-proclaimed mediocre developer. I am lucky to have the charisma, social skills and experience in client work to be able to explain complex topics to technical and non-technical stakeholders but there's room to improve, especially if and when it comes when I have to present in person.

## First Impressions

Before I showed up, my biggest concern was how empty it would be, given that there's four Toastmasters clubs within a fifteen kilometre radius of me. I also showed up late to a surprise large room with around 25 people sat in a U shape desk structure. The person closest to the door immediately saw that I was new, handed me a piece of paper and asked me to write my name and sit wherever I wanted. I sat at the opposite edge of the room, closest to the podium. As soon as I sat down, the headmaster? asked me to introduce myself. This I was pretty comfortable with.

I noticed that there was two different types of nametags at everyone's desks, hand-written and printed. It was clear that the hand-written was for guests where as printed was for club members. At every guests' introduction, everyone clapped. Across from me, a man had a large traffic light on his desk.

Another thing I noticed was how diverse the room was culturally. Well, this might be just a Canada thing, but there were both newcomers and long timers in the room. I did notice and guess that I was one of the youngest in the room, and I would say the majority of the room was probably millenials or Gen X'ers. There was definitely one or two retirees.

## Table Topics

I read the welcome guide ahead of time, so I knew that there was a "Table Topics" section, where you're expected to practice imprompto speaking. I didn't realize you had to do the speaking challenge in front of the whole room. At the front of the room they had a container with small sheets of paper. The headmaster asks for volunteers (guests included, but I didn't go because I wanted to listen first... And I was honestly nervous).
